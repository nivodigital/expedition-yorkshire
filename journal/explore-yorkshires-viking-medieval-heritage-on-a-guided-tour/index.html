Yorkshire offers far more than scenic landscapes — it holds a rich tapestry of history, from Viking settlers to medieval lords. The region was a pivotal place in the Viking Age, where Norse settlers profoundly shaped its culture, language and landscape, and its castles, abbeys and market towns preserve centuries of heritage. With so many interconnected sites, a guided tour provides the context and insider knowledge that turn a visit into an immersive journey through time.

Top Viking sites to explore

York: the heart of Viking history

Known as Jorvik in the Viking era, York is one of England's most historically significant cities. A visit typically takes in the Jorvik Viking Centre — a reconstructed 9th-century settlement — along with the cobbled lanes and city walls where Vikings lived and traded, and museums whose weapons, jewellery and tools bring the era vividly to life. A good guide illuminates the human stories behind the artefacts: how Vikings lived, traded, fought and settled.

The Viking coastline and moors

Scarborough's coastline still bears traces of Viking activity, with historic fortifications and lookout points, while the North York Moors hold archaeological remains and ancient routes used by Norse settlers — from burial sites to early settlements, and the Norse influence still visible in Yorkshire's place names.

Medieval heritage highlights

Castles and fortifications

  • Skipton Castle — a remarkably complete medieval fortress, with original walls, battlements and chambers.
  • Helmsley Castle — a dramatic ruin set in parkland, rich with tales of royal intrigue and siege.
  • Bolton Castle — a Wensleydale stronghold with stories of imprisonment and local legend, a highlight of our Dales, castles & villages tour.

Abbeys and churches

  • Fountains Abbey — a UNESCO World Heritage Cistercian monastery and one of England's greatest ruins; read more in our guide to Fountains Abbey.
  • Rievaulx Abbey — atmospheric ruins set in wooded valley, telling of the abbey's rise, wealth and decline.
  • Selby Abbey — one of England's finest medieval abbey churches, still in use today.

Why a guided heritage tour makes the difference

History needs context to come alive. A knowledgeable guide turns dates and names into compelling stories, helping you picture Viking York or life inside a medieval castle. Guided tours also open the door to lesser-known sites, keep you safe on uneven ground and cliff paths, and create an immersion that self-guided travel simply can't match. Whether you are a history enthusiast, a family after an educational adventure, or a traveller wanting something different, Yorkshire's Viking and medieval heritage offers a journey through time you won't soon forget.
